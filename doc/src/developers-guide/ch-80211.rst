.. _dg:cha:80211:

The IEEE 802.11 Model
=====================

This chapter lays down the architecture of the IEEE 802.11 model for a
developer who reads, extends or replaces parts of it. It says what each part
is for, which contract it implements, and how the parts fit together. It does
not describe every class. The
:doc:`User's Guide chapter on 802.11 </users-guide/ch-80211>` says how to
configure the model. The :doc:`chapter on the physical layer <ch-physicallayer>`
describes the generic wireless framework that the 802.11 radio builds on.

The chapter describes the intended design of the model. Where the code does
not meet it yet, the last section, :ref:`dg:sec:80211:gaps`, says so.

The chapter has five parts. The first gives the map of the model. The second
walks the map, cell by cell, along the path of a frame. The third gives the
four properties that hold in every cell: state, communication, time and
observation. The fourth says where to plug in. The fifth says where the code
differs from the design.

.. _dg:sec:80211:glance:

At a glance
-----------

.. _dg:sec:80211:goals:

Design goals
~~~~~~~~~~~~

The model has two goals that pull in different directions. It must be a
full-featured, validated model of the standard. And it must let a researcher
experiment with one aspect of the protocol without a risk to the validity of
the rest. The architecture answers with three rules:

-  **Every function of the standard is its own part.** Channel access, frame
   exchange, acknowledgement, retry, fragmentation, aggregation, block
   acknowledgement, rate selection and rate control each live in one module
   or one class with one responsibility. A change to one part does not touch
   the others.

-  **Every part that has more than one reasonable implementation is a
   replaceable slot.** The slot is typed by a contract interface, and the ini
   file selects the implementation. A user of one implementation is not
   affected by a change to another one.

-  **Channel access is separate from frame exchange, and transmission is
   separate from reception.** The contention process only answers "you may
   transmit now". The frame exchange only says "send this, then expect that".
   The transmitter only sends one frame after an inter-frame space. This
   keeps every state machine small.

.. _dg:sec:80211:map:

The map
~~~~~~~

Two axes divide the model into parts. The **layer** is the position in the
stack: LLC, MAC, PHY, and the medium below them. The **plane** is the kind of
work. The *data plane* carries MSDUs, frame by frame, on a time scale of
microseconds to milliseconds. The *management plane* sets up and keeps the
relation to a BSS: scan, authentication, association and beacons, on a scale
of seconds. The standard draws the same line between the MAC data service
and the management entities. The code draws it too: management frames have
their own gate pair on the MAC.

Every module of the model falls into one cell of the grid.

.. _dg:sec:80211:patterns:

Three patterns in every cell
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Three patterns repeat inside the cells. They are not parts. They explain why
parts come in pairs and in families, and they are the columns of the tables
in the next part.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 40 42

   * - Pattern
     - Meaning
     - Examples
   * - direction
     - Every layer has an originator side and a recipient side. In the
       management plane the station initiates and the access point responds.
     - encapsulate and decapsulate; the originator and recipient data
       services; ``tx`` and ``rx``; transmitter and receiver
   * - decision and mechanism
     - A part decides, or a part executes. Almost every replaceable slot is
       on the decision side.
     - the agent decides, the management module executes; a policy decides,
       a procedure executes; rate control decides, rate selection applies
   * - variant
     - Parts come in families by generation or by fidelity.
     - DCF and EDCA; the non-QoS and QoS class pairs; PHY families from DSSS
       to VHT; radios from unit disk to bit level; full and simplified
       management

The MAC data plane divides further by **function**: access, exchange,
transformation, reliability, and rate and protection. Each function answers
one question, and each has an originator side, a recipient side, and a DCF
and an EDCA variant. The section on the MAC follows this axis.

.. _dg:sec:80211:parts:

The parts, cell by cell
-----------------------

This part walks the grid. The data plane comes first, from the top down, in
the order a frame takes: the interface, the LLC, the MAC with its five
functions, the PHY, the medium. The management plane follows. The two parts
across layers come last. Every section names the contract of each part; the
contracts view in the next part lists all contracts in one place.

.. _dg:sec:80211:interface:

The network interface
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

:ned:`Ieee80211Interface` is a composition. It names seven slots, wires
them, and fans out the parameters that more than one slot needs
(:par:`opMode`, :par:`bitrate`, the module paths). It has no behaviour of its
own beyond what :ned:`NetworkInterface` gives every interface: the interface
entry, the address, and the up or down state.

.. graphviz:: figures/ieee80211_interface.dot
   :align: center

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 15 33 52

   * - Slot
     - Contract
     - Responsibility
   * - ``llc``
     - ``IIeee80211Llc`` (NED and C++)
     - Encapsulates the payload for the wireless medium. The C++ contract has
       one method, ``getProtocol()``, which tells the MAC which protocol to
       tag a decapsulated frame with.
   * - ``classifier``
     - ``IIeee8021dQosClassifier``
     - Adds a ``UserPriorityReq`` tag to a packet on the transmit path.
   * - ``agent``
     - ``IIeee80211Agent``
     - The station management entity: decides when to scan and which access
       point to join. Optional.
   * - ``mgmt``
     - ``IIeee80211Mgmt``
     - The MLME: runs the management procedures, owns their timers, and
       writes the association state into the MIB.
   * - ``mac``
     - ``IIeee80211Mac``
     - The MAC sublayer.
   * - ``radio``
     - ``IRadio``
     - The PHY.
   * - ``mib``
     - none
     - The shared state of the interface. Fixed type.

Three paths cross the interface, and they do not mix. Data packets go
through the LLC and the MAC. Management frame bodies go between the
management module and the MAC on their own gate pair. Radio commands go from
the management module through the MAC to the radio.

.. _dg:sec:80211:llc:

The LLC
~~~~~~~

The LLC cell holds the classifier and the LLC. Both sit on the data plane
between the network layer and the MAC.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 26 20 54

   * - Part
     - Variant of
     - What it does
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211LlcLpd`
     - LLC, the default
     - Adds an IEEE 802.2 LLC/SNAP header; the SNAP form carries the
       EtherType.
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211LlcEpd`
     - LLC, for 802.11p
     - Adds an EtherType header.
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211Portal`
     - LLC, in an access point
     - Converts between 802.11 frames and Ethernet frames, so that the relay
       unit of the access point can bridge them.
   * - :ned:`QosClassifier` and the other classifiers
     - classifier
     - Maps a packet to a user priority and adds the ``UserPriorityReq`` tag.
       Omitted by default.

The LLC variants share the one-method contract ``IIeee80211Llc``. Nothing on
the wire says which LLC encapsulated a frame, so the MAC records it in an
``LlcProtocolTag``.

.. _dg:sec:80211:mac:

The MAC
~~~~~~~

:ned:`Ieee80211Mac` is a compound module with an active C++ class. The
class is the frame codec and the dispatcher; the submodules do the protocol.

.. graphviz:: figures/ieee80211_mac.dot
   :align: center

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 15 31 54

   * - Part
     - Contract
     - Responsibility
   * - the MAC class
     - ``IIeee80211Mac`` (NED)
     - Splits messages by gate and kind. Encapsulates a packet into a data
       frame with the address roles that the MIB mode and station type
       dictate, and decapsulates a received frame into a packet with
       indication tags. Builds the header of a management frame body. Hands
       every frame to the active coordination function. Relays the radio
       state signals to ``rx`` and ``tx``. Registers the interface entry. Owns
       no protocol state.
   * - ``rx``
     - ``IRx``
     - Checks the FCS of every received frame. Owns the NAV. Derives
       *medium free* from the reception state, the transmission state and the
       NAV, and tells every registered contention when it changes. Reports a
       corrupt frame so that contentions switch to EIFS.
   * - ``tx``
     - ``ITx``
     - Transmits one frame after a given inter-frame space, unconditionally.
       Fills the transmitter address and the FCS. Calls back the requester
       when the radio has finished, and extends the NAV by the frame's
       duration field.
   * - ``ds``
     - ``IDs``
     - The distribution service: for a received data frame, decides between
       pass up, forward into the BSS, or both, from the station table in the
       MIB.
   * - ``dcf``
     - ``IDcf`` (NED), ``ICoordinationFunction``
     - The coordination function of a non-QoS station.
   * - ``hcf``
     - ``IHcf`` (NED), ``ICoordinationFunction``
     - The coordination function of a QoS station. Present only when
       :par:`qosStation` is true, and then it serves every frame.

The MAC controls the radio mode: it switches the radio to transmitter mode
before a frame goes down, and back to receiver mode after the transmission.
The coordination function asks for the switch when it starts and ends a
transmission, so that the mode change is part of the frame exchange and not
a side effect of a signal.

A coordination function is the unit that a developer replaces to change how
the station accesses the medium and exchanges frames. Its contract towards
the MAC is ``ICoordinationFunction``: ``processUpperFrame``,
``processLowerFrame`` and ``corruptedFrameReceived``. Towards its own parts
it implements the callback interfaces ``IChannelAccess::ICallback``,
``ITx::ICallback``, ``IFrameSequenceHandler::ICallback`` and
``IProcedureCallback``. Everything below this line is inside one
coordination function, and it divides along the five functions of the data
plane. The figure shows the parts in five bands; only the calls between the
bands are drawn, and the sections say who calls whom inside a band.

.. graphviz:: figures/ieee80211_coordination_function.dot
   :align: center

Two patterns run through all five bands. A *policy* answers a question and
holds no frame state: is an ACK needed, is an RTS needed, may this RTS be
answered with a CTS, which ack policy applies, when is a block ack agreement
wanted. Every policy is a replaceable module typed by an ``I...Policy``
interface. The policies are a concept of this model, not of the standard: the
standard states the rules, and the model separates each rule into a slot so
that an experiment can replace it. A *procedure* builds the frames of one
mechanism and keeps the state of that mechanism: the RTS, CTS and recipient
ACK procedures, and the block ack agreement handlers and procedures. A
procedure is a C++ object owned by the coordination function, and it hands a
frame to send back to the coordination function through
``IProcedureCallback``.

Access
^^^^^^

Access decides *when* the station may transmit.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 22 42 36

   * - Part
     - Responsibility
     - DCF and EDCA
   * - channel access (``IChannelAccess``)
     - Owns the pending queue, the in-progress frames and the contention
       window of one access function. Reports the grant to the coordination
       function through ``IChannelAccess::ICallback``.
     - :ned:`Dcaf` is the single access function of DCF. :ned:`Edca` holds one
       :ned:`Edcaf` per access category.
   * - contention (``IContention``)
     - The state machine that waits for a free medium, an inter-frame space
       and a backoff. It learns the medium state from ``rx`` and reports the
       grant through ``IContention::ICallback``.
     - One per access function.
   * - collision controller
     - Settles an internal collision between two access categories in favour
       of the higher one (``IEdcaCollisionController``).
     - EDCA only.
   * - TXOP procedure
     - The transmit opportunity: its start, its limit per access category,
       and the time left, which bounds a frame exchange.
     - EDCA only, one per access category.
   * - the NAV and the medium state
     - The recipient side of access: ``rx`` keeps the NAV from the duration
       fields it receives and derives the medium state.
     - Shared by all access functions.

The contention window changes only on request of the recovery procedure,
through the ``ICwCalculator`` callback that the channel access implements.

Exchange
^^^^^^^^

Exchange decides *what* the station sends and expects once it has the
channel. A frame exchange is a grammar of steps: a transmit step sends a
frame, a receive step waits for a frame of an expected type within a
timeout. The combinators ``SequentialFs``, ``AlternativesFs``,
``RepeatingFs`` and ``OptionalFs`` compose primitive sequences into
``DcfFs``, ``HcfFs`` and ``TxOpFs``. This form maps the frame exchange
sequences of Annex G to code one step at a time, and it keeps retransmission
out of the sequence: a failed step ends the sequence, and the recovery
procedure decides what happens to the frame.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 24 40 36

   * - Part
     - Responsibility
     - DCF and EDCA
   * - frame sequence handler
     - Runs one sequence at a time (``IFrameSequenceHandler``): asks the
       sequence for the next step,
       transmits through ``ITx`` or arms the receive timer, and reports each
       accepted or rejected step to the coordination function through its
       callback.
     - One per coordination function.
   * - frame sequences (``IFrameSequence``)
     - The grammar. The primitive sequences are data, management, RTS, CTS,
       fragment with ACK, last fragment with ACK, block ack request with
       block ack.
     - ``DcfFs``: RTS/CTS, fragments, ACKs. ``HcfFs`` repeats ``TxOpFs``
       rounds within the TXOP: data with ACK, data under block ack, block ack
       request, management with ACK.
   * - frame sequence context
     - What one exchange needs: the in-progress frames, the policies, the
       procedures, and for a QoS station the TXOP procedure.
     - Built by the coordination function per grant.
   * - the procedures
     - Build an RTS for a pending frame; answer a received RTS with a CTS
       and a received frame with an ACK; build and answer a block ack
       request.
     - The block ack procedures exist in EDCA only.

Transformation
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Transformation turns MSDUs into MPDUs and back, as the MAC data plane of
clause 5.1.5 prescribes. The originator service
(``IOriginatorMacDataService``) takes packets from the pending queue and
produces the frames to transmit. The recipient service
(``IRecipientMacDataService``) reverses the steps. The in-progress frames
pull from the originator service only when they hold no frame that may be
transmitted, so the queue keeps packets in their original form as long as
possible.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 35 35

   * - Step, in order
     - Originator
     - Recipient, in reverse order
   * - aggregation of MSDUs
     - A-MSDU, by ``IMsduAggregationPolicy`` (EDCA)
     - A-MSDU deaggregation
   * - sequence number
     - per receiver and TID in EDCA, one counter in DCF
     - duplicate removal per transmitter, and per TID in EDCA
   * - fragmentation
     - by ``IFragmentationPolicy``
     - reassembly
   * - reordering
     - none
     - block ack reordering per agreement (EDCA)
   * - aggregation of MPDUs
     - A-MPDU, by ``IMpduAggregationPolicy`` (EDCA)
     - A-MPDU deaggregation

Reliability
^^^^^^^^^^^

Reliability answers: did the frame arrive, and what if not?

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 24 40 36

   * - Part
     - Responsibility
     - DCF and EDCA
   * - ack handler (``IAckHandler``)
     - Owns the acknowledgement status of every in-progress frame. The
       in-progress frames ask it which frame may be transmitted next.
     - Keyed by sequence control in DCF; by receiver, TID and sequence
       control in EDCA, one per access category.
   * - recovery procedure
     - Owns the retry counters, short and long, per frame and per station
       (``IRecoveryProcedure``).
       Decides when the retry limit is reached, and asks the channel access
       to double or reset the contention window.
     - One in DCF. Each EDCAF has a QoS-data procedure and a separate
       management/non-QoS procedure, both bound to that EDCAF's CW.
   * - block ack agreement handlers and procedures
     - Set up, keep and tear down the agreements per peer and TID, on both
       sides; build and answer the block ack request.
     - EDCA only.
   * - the ack, RTS, CTS and agreement policies
     - Whether an ACK is needed, the timeouts, whether an RTS protects a
       frame, whether a CTS answers, whether an agreement is wanted.
     - Non-QoS and QoS variants of each.

HCF selects management recovery through
``edcaf->getMgmtAndNonQoSRecoveryProcedure()``. The internal-collision path
selects the losing EDCAF; the exchange paths select the exchange EDCAF.
For CTS, the preceding RTS step identifies the protected frame. Management
ACK, CTS and multicast completion use the same local procedure as failure.
The module hierarchy owns the procedure; the EDCAF's pointer is borrowed.
Both recovery procedures bind their CW calculator during the last
initialization stage, after the EDCAF initializes CW in the link-layer stage.

Each ``edca.edcaf[i].mgmtAndNonQoSRecoveryProcedure`` publishes its own
``contentionWindowChanged`` and ``retryLimitReached`` signals. Filter by
source/AC; ancestor subscriptions receive several independent streams,
including each procedure's initial CW sample.

The coordination function connects them: on an acknowledgement it resets the
counters and drops the frame, on a failure it counts and either marks the
retry bit or drops the frame.

Rate and protection
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The rate selection (``IRateSelection``, ``IQosRateSelection``) picks the PHY
mode of every transmitted frame and of every response frame, from the
configured bit rates, the mode set, the peer's capabilities in the MIB, and
the optional rate control. Rate control (``IRateControl``) adapts the data
rate per receiver from the success and failure feedback that the
coordination function gives it. The protection mechanism computes the
duration field of every frame from the same rate selection, so that the NAV
of the other stations covers the rest of the exchange. EDCA adds the block
ack response rate and a TXOP-aware rule for control frames.

A frame's journey
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

On the transmit side: the LLC hands a packet to the MAC; the MAC builds the
data frame and gives it to the coordination function; the coordination
function enqueues it and requests the channel; the contention grants; the
coordination function starts a frame sequence; the sequence's transmit step
takes the next frame from the in-progress frames, which pull it through the
originator data service; the coordination function selects the mode and the
duration and gives the frame to ``tx``; ``tx`` waits the inter-frame space
and hands it to the MAC; the MAC switches the radio and sends it down.

On the receive side: the radio delivers the packet; the MAC gives it to
``rx``, which checks the FCS and updates the NAV; the MAC gives it to the
coordination function; the coordination function routes it either into the
frame sequence in progress or to the recipient side, where the procedures
answer with an ACK or a CTS and the recipient data service produces complete
frames; the coordination function sends each complete frame up; the MAC
decapsulates it and passes a management frame to the management module and
a data frame to ``ds``, which passes it up or forwards it.

.. _dg:sec:80211:phy:

The physical layer
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

:ned:`Ieee80211Radio` builds on the flat radio of the generic framework, which
the :doc:`chapter on the physical layer <ch-physicallayer>` describes. The
generic framework gives the radio modes, the transmission and reception
timers, the medium, the propagation and path loss models, the analog signal
representations and the reception decision. The 802.11 model adds five
things.

-  **Modes and mode sets.** ``IIeee80211Mode`` describes one PHY mode:
   its preamble, header and data submodes with their bit rates and
   durations, and the time constants of the PHY, such as slot time, SIFS,
   CCA time and receive start delay. ``Ieee80211ModeSet`` is the immutable
   table of modes of one operation mode, with the mandatory flag of each. The
   MAC takes its time constants from the reference mode of the set, and it
   publishes the set with ``modesetChanged`` so that every part uses the same
   one.

-  **Bands and channels.** ``IIeee80211Band`` maps a channel index to a
   center frequency; ``Ieee80211Channel`` is a band with an index. The
   transmitter and the receiver set their center frequency from the channel.

-  **The PHY header.** One chunk class per PHY family
   (``Ieee80211PhyHeader.msg``). The radio prepends it on transmit and pops
   it on receive, and the transmitter reads its length field to compute the
   duration.

-  **Transmitter and receiver.** ``Ieee80211Transmitter`` picks the mode
   from the ``Ieee80211ModeReq`` tag and creates an ``Ieee80211Transmission``
   that carries the mode and the channel. ``Ieee80211Receiver`` admits a
   transmission only when its mode is in the receiver's mode set, and adds
   the mode and channel indications.

-  **Error models.** ``Ieee80211ErrorModelBase`` computes the packet error
   rate from the success rate of the header and of the data part, each for
   the modulation and code of its submode. :ned:`Ieee80211NistErrorModel`
   and :ned:`Ieee80211YansErrorModel` give the curves;
   :ned:`Ieee80211BerTableErrorModel` reads them from a file.

The variants follow the two patterns of the PHY: fidelity and family.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 22 78

   * - Axis
     - Variants
   * - fidelity
     - :ned:`Ieee80211UnitDiskRadio` (range only, no error model),
       :ned:`Ieee80211ScalarRadio` (one power per signal),
       :ned:`Ieee80211DimensionalRadio` (power over time and frequency),
       :ned:`Ieee80211OfdmRadio` (bit level: encoder, interleaver, modulator
       and their inverses). All produce an ``Ieee80211Transmission`` and
       speak the same tags to the MAC.
   * - family
     - DSSS, HR/DSSS, OFDM, ERP-OFDM, HT, VHT, each a mode class family and a
       PHY header class; FHSS and IR exist but no mode set uses them.

.. _dg:sec:80211:medium:

The medium
~~~~~~~~~~

The radio medium is one module per network, shared by every radio. A radio
registers with it at the physical-layer stage and finds it through the
``radioMediumModule`` parameter. To transmit, the radio calls
``transmitPacket``; the medium sends a ``WirelessSignal`` by ``sendDirect``
to the ``radioIn`` gate of every radio that can receive it. During a
reception the radio asks the medium whether the reception is attempted and
successful, and the medium asks the receiver. :ned:`Ieee80211RadioMedium`
adds no code; it sets 802.11 defaults for the background noise and the
range limits.

.. _dg:sec:80211:mgmt:

The management module
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The management module is the MLME. It runs the procedures of the standard:
scan, authentication, association, reassociation, beacons, and the beacon
timeout. It owns every timer of these procedures. It builds management frame
bodies and reads received ones. It writes the results into the MIB:
``mode``, ``bssStationData``, ``bssData`` and, on an access point, the
station table and the association identifiers. It emits the association
signals. It never decides *whether* to scan or *which* access point to join.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 14 56

   * - Variant
     - Role
     - Procedures it runs
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211MgmtSta`
     - station
     - scan, authentication, association, reassociation, beacon timeout;
       needs an agent
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211MgmtAp`
     - access point
     - beacons, probe responses, authentication, association, the station
       table and the association identifiers
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211MgmtStaSimplified`, :ned:`Ieee80211MgmtApSimplified`
     - station, access point
     - none; the station writes itself as associated into its own MIB and
       into the MIB of its access point
   * - :ned:`Ieee80211MgmtAdhoc`
     - ad hoc
     - none; sets the independent mode

An access point never starts a procedure. It reserves an association
identifier when it builds a response and commits it only when the
coordination function reports the response as acknowledged, through
``frameTransmissionOutcome``. The station table in the MIB is what ``ds``
and the MAC consult to forward or drop a frame.

The five variants share the ``IIeee80211Mgmt`` contract: the gates to the
MAC and to the agent, and the MIB fields above. A new variant must keep the
MIB fields true, because the MAC and ``ds`` act on them. The management
module runs its procedures through the MAC like any other client: frame
bodies on the management gates, radio commands for the channel changes of a
scan, and the ``receptionStateChanged`` signal of the radio to detect a busy
channel during an active scan.

.. _dg:sec:80211:agent:

The agent
~~~~~~~~~

The agent is the station management entity. It decides, and it drives the
management module with the MLME primitives: scan, then authenticate with the
chosen access point, then associate; and scan again when the beacon is lost.
A different join strategy, a handover policy or a scripted behaviour is a
different agent, and the management module does not change. The agent
speaks the primitives only; it needs no other knowledge of the management
module.

.. _dg:sec:80211:plme:

Radio configuration
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The PLME of the model is the command interface of the radio. A
``Request`` message with kind ``RADIO_C_CONFIGURE`` and an
``Ieee80211ConfigureRadioCommand`` control info changes the PHY mode, the
mode set, the band, the channel or the bit rate. The management module sends
it on its ``macOut`` gate for the channel changes of a scan. The MAC forwards
it to the radio, and it holds the command while the medium is busy, so a
channel change never cuts a frame exchange. The radio applies the fields in
order, pushes every change to both the transmitter and the receiver, and
publishes ``radioChannelChanged``, which the access point management uses
to keep the primary channel in the MIB. The MAC uses the base class
``ConfigureRadioCommand`` for its own radio mode switches.

.. _dg:sec:80211:mib:

The MIB
~~~~~~~

The MIB is the one shared object of the interface. A part that needs its
state holds a pointer that it resolves from its ``mibModule`` parameter,
which the interface sets. Half of its fields are public and written
directly: the management module writes the mode, the station type, the BSS
data, the association flag and the station table; the MAC writes the address
and the local HT capabilities. The other half is private and changes only
through the MIB's own operations: the HT operation, the primary channel, the
association identifiers and the peer HT states. Everyone else reads. The
state view in the next part gives every field with its owner, its writers,
its readers and its lifetime.

.. _dg:sec:80211:views:

The views
---------

Five properties hold in every cell. Each gets one view: state, communication,
time, observation, and contracts.

.. _dg:sec:80211:communication:

Communication
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Inside the interface the parts use a fixed set of mechanisms. A developer
who adds a part chooses from this set.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 24 76

   * - Means
     - Used for
   * - a packet or a command on a gate
     - crosses a layer boundary: LLC to MAC, management to MAC, MAC to
       radio
   * - a direct C++ call through a contract interface
     - joins parts that must act in the same instant: everything inside the
       MAC and the radio
   * - a callback interface
     - returns a result later: channel grants, transmission completions,
       frame sequence events, control responses
   * - a signal
     - publishes a fact that happened; a subscriber may react, the emitter
       does not depend on it: radio state to the MAC, the mode set to the
       MAC parts, the channel change to the access point management,
       association events to the node
   * - a shared object
     - holds state that several parts read: the MIB, the mode set, the
       pending queue and the in-progress frames of a coordination function
   * - a self-message
     - the only way to wait; every part owns the timers it needs

**How a part finds a peer.** The caller finds the callee in one of three ways
only: as its own submodule; by a module path parameter that the parent NED
sets, such as ``*.rxModule = "^.rx"``; or as the neighbour of one of its
gates. A part never searches the module tree for a peer, and a part never
asks a sibling for a pointer to a third module.

**The programming interface.** A module outside the interface talks to the
802.11 model through packets, commands and primitives. It never calls a C++
method of the model.

-  **Data packets.** A packet enters on ``upperLayerIn`` with a
   ``MacAddressReq`` tag (the destination) and a ``PacketProtocolTag`` (the
   payload protocol). A ``UserPriorityReq`` tag, when present, selects the
   access category of a QoS station. A received packet leaves on
   ``upperLayerOut`` with ``MacAddressInd``, ``InterfaceInd``,
   ``PacketProtocolTag`` and, for a QoS frame, ``UserPriorityInd``. See
   :doc:`ch-tags` for the tag mechanism.

-  **Management frames.** A management module sends the *body* of a
   management frame on the ``mgmtIn`` gate of the MAC with three tags:
   ``MacAddressReq`` (the receiver), ``Ieee80211SubtypeReq`` (the frame
   subtype) and ``PacketProtocolTag`` set to ``Protocol::ieee80211Mgmt``. The
   MAC adds the header, the trailer and the transmitter address. A received
   management frame comes back on ``mgmtOut`` with its header popped.

-  **Radio commands.** The ``Ieee80211ConfigureRadioCommand`` of the
   radio configuration section, on the ``macOut`` gate of the management
   module.

-  **MLME primitives.** The agent and the management module exchange the
   primitives of the MLME SAP as ``cMessage`` objects with a control info
   from ``Ieee80211Primitives.msg``. Each request has a confirm with a result
   code. The table omits the ``Ieee80211Prim_`` prefix of the class names.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 30 40

   * - Request
     - Confirm
     - Fields
   * - ``ScanRequest``
     - ``ScanConfirm``
     - BSS type, BSSID, SSID, active or passive, probe delay, channel list,
       minimum and maximum channel time; the confirm carries the list of
       BSS descriptions with their received power
   * - ``AuthenticateRequest``
     - ``AuthenticateConfirm``
     - address, timeout
   * - ``AssociateRequest``, ``ReassociateRequest``
     - ``AssociateConfirm``, ``ReassociateConfirm``
     - address, timeout
   * - ``DeauthenticateRequest``, ``DisassociateRequest``
     - none
     - address, reason code

The result codes are ``PRC_SUCCESS``, ``PRC_INVALID_PARAMETERS``,
``PRC_TIMEOUT``, ``PRC_TOO_MANY_SIMULTANEOUS_REQUESTS`` and ``PRC_REFUSED``.

-  **Packets between the MAC and the radio.** The MAC tags a frame with
   ``Ieee80211ModeReq`` (the PHY mode the rate selection chose) and, when
   needed, ``Ieee80211ChannelReq``. The radio delivers a received frame with
   ``Ieee80211ModeInd``, ``Ieee80211ChannelInd``, ``SignalPowerInd``,
   ``SnirInd``, ``SignalTimeInd`` and ``ErrorRateInd``. A frame that the error
   model rejected arrives with its bit error flag set; the MAC drops it. The
   protocol identifiers are ``Protocol::ieee80211Mac`` for a MAC frame and
   one ``Protocol::ieee80211*Phy`` value per PHY family for a frame with a
   PHY header.

.. _dg:sec:80211:time:

Time
~~~~

The two planes run on two time scales. The data plane acts in microseconds:
slot times, inter-frame spaces, ACK timeouts, frame durations. The
management plane acts in seconds: beacon intervals, scan times, association
timeouts. Every timer of the model belongs to the part that waits on it.

**Initialization.** INET orders initialization by named stages. The file
``src/inet/common/InitStages.cc`` defines the stages and their order. The
802.11 parts use them as follows; the table omits the ``INITSTAGE_`` prefix
of the names.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 42 58

   * - Stage
     - What a part may assume after it
   * - ``LOCAL``
     - Every part has read its own parameters and made its subscriptions.
       The mode set listeners have subscribed to ``modesetChanged``.
   * - ``PHYSICAL_LAYER``
     - The radio is registered with the medium, has its initial mode, and
       has published its channel.
   * - ``NETWORK_INTERFACE_CONFIGURATION``
     - The interface has its address and its interface entry. The MAC has
       copied the address into the MIB. The management module has started.
   * - ``LINK_LAYER``
     - The MAC has found its peers, subscribed to the radio, published the
       mode set and set the radio mode. The coordination function and the
       channel access have found their parts, and the contentions are
       registered with ``rx``.
   * - ``LAST``
     - Subscriptions and lookups that need a part created in an earlier stage
       are done: the coordination function on the pending queues, the
       recovery procedures on their policies.

A part resolves a peer in the earliest stage in which the peer exists and
is initialized. It does not do protocol work in ``initialize``. The first
start of a part is part of its initialization: a module that extends
``OperationalBase`` calls its ``handleStartOperation`` at its own
initialization stage when the node is up.

**Lifecycle.** The lifecycle operations reach the parts that extend
``OperationalBase``: the interface, the MAC, the management module and the
radio. The :doc:`User's Guide chapter on node failures </users-guide/ch-lifecycle>`
says how a scenario starts, stops and crashes a node. The contract per
operation:

-  **Start.** The radio applies its initial mode. The management module
   starts its procedures: an access point schedules its beacon, a station
   scans. The MAC applies the initial radio mode and marks the interface
   up.

-  **Stop.** The MAC drops every queued and in-progress frame with reason
   ``INTERFACE_DOWN``, cancels every timer of its parts, and resets the
   acknowledgement, retry, contention and block ack state, so that a later
   start begins clean. The management module cancels its timers and clears
   the association state. The radio cancels its timers and switches off.
   The interface marks itself down.

-  **Crash.** The same as stop, without any frame sent.

The parts inside the MAC have no lifecycle hooks of their own; the MAC is
responsible for their reset. The agent restarts its scan on a start
operation.

   * - ``IIeee80211Llc``
     - standard
     - LPD, EPD, portal
     - ``llc``
   * - ``IIeee80211Mode`` with its three submodes; ``IIeee80211Band``
     - standard
     - one base each, seven PHY families
     - none

Three facts about the contracts as they are today. Four interfaces are
never called through their C++ type: ``ICoordinationFunction``, ``IDs``,
``IIeee80211Llc`` and ``IRecoveryProcedure``; the first two are reached
through NED slots and gates, and the MAC holds the concrete ``Dcf`` and
``Hcf``. Twenty-two interfaces have one implementation and no slot; all of
them name a mechanism of the standard or a PHY concept, and they stay. Four
NED interface names differ from their C++ names: ``ICollisionController``
against ``IEdcaCollisionController``, ``IDcf`` and ``IHcf`` against
``ICoordinationFunction``, and ``IOriginatorQosAckPolicy`` against
``IOriginatorQoSAckPolicy``.

.. _dg:sec:80211:extension-points:

Extension points
----------------

The slots follow the two patterns that create them. A decision is replaced;
a variant is added.

Decisions to replace
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 24 34 42

   * - To replace
     - Implement, and plug into
     - Notes
   * - a policy: ACK, RTS, CTS, fragmentation, aggregation, block ack
       agreement
     - the ``I...Policy`` C++ interface and NED interface that match; the
       policy slot of ``dcf`` or ``hcf``
     - A policy answers questions and keeps no frame state. It may read the
       mode set and ask the rate selection.
   * - the rate control algorithm
     - ``IRateControl``, best through ``RateControlBase``; the
       ``rateControl`` slot
     - It receives success and failure per frame and returns a mode per
       receiver. It resets on ``modesetChanged``.
   * - the classifier
     - ``IIeee8021dQosClassifier``; the ``classifier`` slot
     - It adds a ``UserPriorityReq`` tag.
   * - the agent
     - ``IIeee80211Agent`` (gates); the ``agent`` slot
     - It speaks the MLME primitives only.

Variants to add
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 24 34 42

   * - To add
     - Implement, and plug into
     - Notes
   * - a frame exchange
     - ``IFrameSequence``, composed from the combinators and the primitive
       sequences
     - The coordination function creates it on a channel grant. Today this
       means a subclass of :ned:`Dcf` or :ned:`Hcf` that overrides the grant
       handler.
   * - a coordination function
     - ``ICoordinationFunction`` plus the four callback interfaces; the
       ``dcf`` or ``hcf`` slot
     - It may reuse the channel access, the data services, the policies and
       the frame sequence handler as submodules or objects.
   * - a channel access or a contention
     - ``IChannelAccess`` or ``IContention``; the ``channelAccess`` slot of
       :ned:`Dcf`, or the ``contention`` slot of :ned:`Dcaf` and :ned:`Edcaf`
     - A contention must register with ``rx`` and report through
       ``IContention::ICallback``.
   * - a management variant
     - ``IIeee80211Mgmt`` (gates), ``Ieee80211MgmtBase`` (dispatch); the
       ``mgmt`` slot
     - It must maintain the MIB fields that the MAC and ``ds`` read.
   * - an LLC
     - ``IIeee80211Llc`` (gates and ``getProtocol()``); the ``llc`` slot
     - It sets ``PacketProtocolTag`` on the way down and the protocol tags of
       the network layer on the way up.
   * - a PHY mode or amendment
     - an ``IIeee80211Mode`` family, an entry in ``Ieee80211ModeSet``, a PHY
       header chunk with its serializer, and error model support
     - The MAC needs only the time constants and the durations from the
       mode; the transmitter and receiver need the header.
   * - an error model
     - ``IErrorModel``, best through ``Ieee80211ErrorModelBase``; the
       ``receiver.errorModel`` slot
     - It gives the success rate of the header and of the data part per
       submode.

When a new interface is allowed
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A new C++ interface must name a concept of the standard, or a decision of
the model with at least one alternative implementation in mind. A need for
information from another module is not a reason for an interface. The peer
lookup rule and a contract that exists, the shared MIB, or a signal serve that
need. A callback is nested in the interface it serves, and it never stands
alone.

The counter-example: a provider interface for the mode set. The mode set is
a catalog datum with one owner, the MAC, and the means that exist distribute
it. An interface that carries it would capture no concept of the model, only
the fact that one part needs a value from another.

.. _dg:sec:80211:gaps:

Where the code differs from this design
---------------------------------------

These points are known. The project document
``doc/project/design/ieee80211-anatomy.md`` describes the code as it was at
one commit, with file references, and it is the place to check the details.

-  **Lifecycle.** The stop and crash hooks of :ned:`Ieee80211Mac` are empty.
   No part inside the MAC resets on a stop; queues, timers, agreements and
   counters survive. The agent has no lifecycle support.

-  **Radio mode.** The MAC switches the radio back to receiver mode from its
   signal handler, not on request of the coordination function.

-  **The coordination function slot.** The MAC holds the concrete ``Dcf``
   and ``Hcf`` and never calls through ``ICoordinationFunction``, so a
   coordination function of another class cannot be plugged in without a
   change to the MAC.

-  **Readiness of the mode set.** The MAC publishes the mode set by one
   signal inside the link-layer stage, and the rate selection uses it in the
   same stage. Whether the set is there depends on the order of the
   siblings.

-  **Separate recovery counter models.** Each EDCAF's QoS-data and
   management/non-QoS procedures retain separate station-counter
   representations and older retry/RTS-threshold rules. Per-AC ownership does
   not imply a complete implementation of the 2024 QSRC/TXOP reset rules.
   RTS exhaustion can also leave a short-retry entry when length-based
   cleanup selects the long-retry map.

-  **A-MPDU aggregation** is present as classes and a policy slot, but the
   call in the originator data service is disabled, and the default policy
   never aggregates. **MSDU lifetime** handlers exist but are never created.
   **Self-CTS** is a stub.

-  **PCF, MCF and HCCA** are placeholders whose entry points throw. The
   ``HtTxOpFs``, ``McfFs`` and ``PcfFs`` sequences are empty and unused.

-  **Two mode sets.** The MAC and the radio each build a mode set from their
   own parameter. The interface wires both from :par:`opMode`; a direct
   override of one desynchronizes them.

-  **Duplicated and stale copies.** The MAC and ``rx`` both cache the
   transmission state of the radio, and ``rx`` keeps a copy of the address
   that no event refreshes.

-  **Management frames** of a QoS station always use the voice access
   category.

-  **Block ack** agreement timeouts mix an absolute time with a delay, and
   the recipient DELBA lookup uses the wrong address. Both are latent because
   the default timeout is zero.

-  **Simplified management.** The simplified access point never fills the
   station table; the simplified station compensates with a direct write
   into the access point's MIB.

-  **Unused state.** The last transmitted mode maps of the rate selections,
   the generation counter of the peer HT state, and two fields of the block
   ack agreements are written and never read, or never written.
