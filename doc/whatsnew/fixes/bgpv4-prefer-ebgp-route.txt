A BGP router prefers a route learned over external BGP to one learned over internal BGP

When two routes to a prefix tied on LOCAL_PREF, AS_PATH length and ORIGIN, the Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) decision process chose the route from the lowest peer address, and skipped the rule of RFC 4271 section 9.1.2.2 d) that prefers a route received via external BGP (EBGP) to one received via internal BGP (IBGP). Two border routers of an autonomous system could each choose the IBGP route of the other and forward packets for the prefix in a loop. A border router that chose the IBGP route also stopped advertising its own EBGP route to the other internal peers, so the LOCAL_PREF it set for them had no effect. In tutorials/bgp Step10, RB1 and RB2 now keep their EBGP routes, and RB3 changes to RB2, as the tutorial describes.
