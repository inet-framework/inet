TcpTahoe and TcpWestwood start one fast retransmit in each window

After a go-back to slow start, TcpTahoe and TcpWestwood sent again segments that the receiver already had, and the duplicate ACKs of these segments started another fast retransmit and another go-back. A transfer could repeat this cycle: in examples/inet/tcpclientserver, Tahoe made 163 fast retransmits in place of 5. Both flavours now keep the variable recover of RFC 6582 section 6, as TcpNewReno does: the third duplicate ACK starts a fast retransmit only when the cumulative ACK covers more than the highest octet that was sent at the last go-back. Tahoe sets recover at its fast retransmit and at a timeout, Westwood at a timeout. A real loss inside a go-back now waits for the retransmission timer. Simulations with TcpTahoe or TcpWestwood and losses change.
