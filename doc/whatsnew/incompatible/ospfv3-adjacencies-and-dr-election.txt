OSPFv3 forms its adjacencies, follows the interface state and elects the DR as RFC 5340 states

OSPFv3 neighbors went from the Exchange state directly to Full when the link state request list was not empty, because a condition was inverted. Topologies with more neighbors and broadcast topologies with more areas, for example examples/ospfv3/multiple_areas_FINAL and examples/ospfv3/v3_mesh, did not converge: neighbors stayed in Loading and sent requests without end. Now an adjacency goes from Exchange through Loading to Full, as RFC 5340 and RFC 2328 state. Link-LSAs of an update leave the request lists of the neighbors, the area finds LSAs of each type, the request and retransmission lists match an LSA by its type, link state id and advertising router, and Inter-Area-Prefix LSAs keep their link state id.

OSPFv3 now follows the state of its interfaces. A carrier loss or an administrative down ends the neighbors of the interface at once and removes the routes through the link, and a carrier return or an administrative up starts the interface again. Before, the interface stayed down for good and its routes did not return.

A router that joins a segment with an established DR no longer waits for the full wait timer, because received Hellos give the BackupSeen event. A Database Exchange that lost its synchronization starts again with BadLSReq (RFC 2328 section 13). The DR is elected again at once when its adjacency ends from inactivity. Some defects of the state machines are corrected too.

All fingerprints of OSPFv3 simulations change, and the trajectories of each OSPFv3 simulation change.
