ECN across IPv6-in-IPv6 tunnels

An Ipv6TunnelInterface sent every tunnel packet with an ECN (Explicit
Congestion Notification) field of Not-ECT (not ECN-Capable Transport), and
the tunnel exit point discarded the ECN field of the outer header. A router
inside a tunnel had nothing to mark, and a Congestion Experienced mark on the
outer header did not reach the destination. The tunnel entry point now copies
the ECN field of the inner header to the outer header, and the exit point
combines the two as RFC 6040 Section 4.2 requires: Congestion Experienced on
the outer header reaches an ECN-capable inner header, and under such an outer
header a datagram that is not ECN-capable is dropped. The DSCP
(Differentiated Services Code Point) of the outer header stays zero, the
default of RFC 2473 Section 6.4. The results of simulations that send
ECN-capable traffic through a tunnel change; the tunnels that Mipv6 and
Pmipv6 create are Ipv6TunnelInterface modules too.
