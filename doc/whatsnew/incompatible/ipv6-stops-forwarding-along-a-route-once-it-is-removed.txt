IPv6 stops forwarding along a route once it is removed or changed

Ipv6 looks a destination up in the destination cache of Ipv6RoutingTable
before it looks at the routes, and the routing table purged that cache when
a route was added but not when one was removed. A node kept forwarding to
the next hop of a removed route until the next route was added anywhere in
its table, until the interface went down, or, for a route with an expiry
time, until that time passed. Removing a route now purges the cache, and
so does a change to the destination, prefix length, metric, next hop or
interface of a route in the table.

Simulations whose IPv6 routes are removed or changed while traffic flows
are affected, for example a Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) withdrawal or a
route deleted or changed from C++ code. Traffic to a destination that is
no longer routed is now answered with an Internet Control Message Protocol
for IPv6 (ICMPv6) Destination Unreachable at the router that lost the
route, instead of being forwarded towards the old next hop.
