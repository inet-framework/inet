The corruption modes decide the errors of each signal region first

In the chunk, byte, and bit corruption modes of an error model, a flat receiver no longer samples the errors a second time after its decision, again and again until some error occurs. The decisions of the header and the data now decide each chunk of their part one time: a chunk fails if its SNIR is not above the SNIR threshold of the receiver, or else if one random draw against its error rate fails, both with the SNIR of the time of that chunk. The received packet gets the failed chunks of the data: an incorrect copy of each failed chunk in the chunk mode, and a raw chunk with flipped bytes or bits, marked incorrect, in the byte and bit modes. The flipped units follow the bit error rate of the chunk, and at least one unit is flipped. In these three modes, only a failed header gives a packet-level bit error, so interference over the end of a frame marks only the late chunks incorrect. The packet mode keeps its results. ApskReceiver, Ieee802154NarrowbandReceiver, and Ieee80211Receiver support all four modes. IErrorModel::computeCorruptedPacket(snir) goes away, with the functions corruptPacket(), corruptChunks(), corruptBytes(), corruptBits(), computeCorruptedPacket(packet, ber), and hasProbabilisticError() of ErrorModelBase.
