IEEE 802.11 channel access: TXOP protection, NAV, recovery and QoS DATA lifetime

With single protection, the default of the new TxopProcedure parameter protectionMechanism, HCF now checks the complete cost of a continuation when Block Ack support is off. Before the next SIFS, the cost of the exchange must be less than the remaining TXNAV, and the initial SIFS and the exchange must fit in a positive TXOP limit. A refused frame waits for a new grant, without a retry increment or failed-rate feedback, and HCF keeps the admitted DATA mode through RTS/CTS. The first exchange keeps a compatibility fallback, also under a positive limit that is too small, and a zero limit permits no more exchanges. Block Ack support turns the new admission check off, also without an agreement. TXOP-based fragmentation is not supported. A continuation that needs RTS/CTS is refused before the RTS, but an initial exchange can still use RTS/CTS. Rx keeps the shared TXNAV apart from the received NAV. TXNAV blocks contention also after the end of the TXOP, but it does not prevent an eligible CTS response, and DCF keeps its local NAV. HCF also blocks local contention during its active sequence, so there is no second grant while it waits for the first ACK. Single protection leaves out the current ACK estimate for group management frames and non-QoS DATA. TxOpFs publishes the ACK policy before the RTS, so when Block Ack replaces an old Normal Ack policy, the RTS field leaves out the ACK and its SIFS. TxopProcedure::getRemaining() now gives the remaining time of the TXOP; before, it gave the elapsed time, so the admission checks of HCF compared with the wrong value.

The new value protectionMechanism = "multiple" selects multiple protection for EDCA TXOPs. The first step adds no interframe space, and the later steps use the SIFS of the mode set. For example, a PPDU of 200 us in a TXOP of 1024 us advertises a reservation of 824 us. Multiple protection stops when the next exchange cannot use a legal Duration field. Multiple protection uses the shared TXNAV, and it does not check a complete exchange against the remaining budget. The IEEE 802.11 chapter of the User's Guide explains the timing and the limits.

CTS, ACK and Basic Block Ack responses keep their Duration fields, but they no longer extend the local NAV of the recipient; received reservations still control channel access. So a recipient can answer another RTS of the same holder. HCF clamps response durations to zero, and rounds them up to whole microseconds. Rx gives local evidence of the interframe space on accepted frames. The detector uses coverage class zero, and a gap outside its local tolerance gives no evidence. A radio command now waits until Tx completes an accepted transmission, including the SIFS before a response.

A management failure now changes the contention window and the retry state of its own access category; before, it changed the best-effort window, also for voice management frames. The hcf.edca.mgmtAndNonQoSRecoveryProcedure module therefore moves below each edcaf[i], and its C++ getter moves from Edca to Edcaf: update the configuration, the signal subscriptions and the result paths. The trajectories of management recovery can change, also the negotiation of Block Ack agreements; the fingerprints of the MacQosWithBlockAck example change, and those of the QoS and NonQos showcases do not. QoS recovery keeps separate retry counters for each receiver. An access category without a frame no longer takes part in an internal collision, so expired voice DATA no longer makes valid video DATA lose. Two crashes in the retry accounting of management frames and in the grant callback of Contention are corrected.

HCF now discards QoS DATA when its finite lifetime expires, with Block Ack support on or off. Each access category has the new parameter mac.hcf.edca.edcaf[*].msduLifetime, 0.512 s by default, which starts when the MAC accepts the frame, so DATA can expire in the queue. Another positive and finite value is allowed. A-MSDU construction starts the timer of the aggregate again, and fragmentation, retransmission and ACK policy changes keep the deadline. HCF records these drops apart from queue overflow and retry limit drops, as packetDropLifetimeExpired. Congested QoS simulations can therefore send newer DATA, for example the Block Ack fragmentation showcase, the QoS showcase and the MacQos examples.

These changes, and the separate expiry event of TXNAV, can change the results and the fingerprints of IEEE 802.11 simulations. They require the modification of C++ code that implements MAC parts outside INET: ITx has the new method hasTransmission(), which is true while Tx waits for a transmission and while it transmits, and both transmitFrame() overloads take an explicit updateLocalNav (true for DCF holder frames, false for HCF holder frames and recipient responses); IRx has invalidateInterframeHistory() and the TXNAV operations; frame sequence callbacks get a start notification and a Boolean submission result; a custom radio must give local endpoint and completion indications; a custom ACK policy must not depend on the second policy query of HCF, which is removed. See the migration guide.
