Simulation results that change, and why

Many corrections of this release change simulation trajectories on purpose. This entry lists the models whose results change, so that a user whose numbers differ after the upgrade can find the reason. The entries above give the details. The fingerprint and statistical baselines of INET record each of these changes.

TCP: each simulation with TCP changes. The defaults of Tcp follow the RFCs (SACK, timestamps, window scaling, delayed ACKs, Limited Transmit, the advertised window, the MSS of the address family, the initial window, the initial RTO, RACK-TLP, PRR, PSH and path MTU discovery), and about 40 repairs of the RFC behavior also reach the algorithms of INET 4.7. LegacyTcp keeps the old defaults, but not the old defects.

IEEE 802.11: each simulation with IEEE 802.11 can change. The frames on the wire follow the standard; HCF follows the rules of IEEE 802.11-2024 for TXOP protection, NAV, recovery, Block Ack and the lifetime of QoS DATA; the HT associations and the rate control per receiver change the selected rates. The carrier of the "5 GHz" band moves by 2.5 MHz, and the ac configurations move to 5180 MHz. The new default maxTransmissionDuration of Ieee80211RadioMedium (35 ms) moves the event fingerprints (tplx) of 215 runs, but not their trajectories. The charts of the QoS, fragmentation and aggregation showcases change for HCF; DCF does not change.

IPv6, Mobile IPv6 and PMIPv6: Neighbor Discovery answers each Router Solicitation, keeps the minimum delay between Router Advertisements, accepts advertisements without a prefix, and sends no probe when Duplicate Address Detection is off. The first home registration of Mobile IPv6 sends one Binding Update. ICMP and ICMPv6 send no errors about broadcast or multicast frames. The MLD, MIPv6, PMIPv6, OSPFv3, PIM and GPSR examples over IPv6 change.

Routing: the RIP and RIPng timers follow RFC 2453 and RFC 2080, and the RIP header layout follows RFC 2453. OSPFv3 forms its adjacencies and follows the interface state. MPLS follows RFC 3032, and RSVP-TE finds its peers by default.

Ethernet, MRP, TSN and gPTP: half-duplex Ethernet drops a frame after 16 collisions. MRP frames carry the address of the bridge, so the election of an automatic manager changes. gPTP sends its Follow_Up after the end of the Sync transmission; the accuracy of the synchronization does not change. The wire formats of MRP, CFM and LLC change only the packet data fingerprints (~tND).

Queueing: RedDropper and StatisticalRateLimiter draw their random numbers in advance, and a filter in pull mode drops at other times, so the DiffServ, DCTCP, NetPerfMeter, queueing tutorial and TSN policing results change with the same statistical behavior.

Physical layer: DielectricObstacleLoss computes the loss of a plane wave, which halves the dielectric loss in dB for the INET materials. A protocol-element receiver keeps the bit error of a packet, which changes the FRER showcase frerandcs. LMac drops a data frame that does not fit in its slot, so the runs of showcases/wireless/sensornetwork with short slots end normally.

QUIC, DHCP and ICMP: a QUIC server pads its Initial datagram to 1200 octets, which changes each QUIC example and showcase. DHCP probes the granted address and retransmits as RFC 2131 states.

Some baselines changed without a change of the model. The CI now runs on Ubuntu 26.04 with clang 21, which changed the last digits of the error-rate histograms of 11 wireless tests and the gate schedule that z3 finds for showcases/tsn/combiningfeatures/gsandcs. 35 configurations, for example 22 QUIC examples and the steps of tutorials/protocol, have a real sim-time-limit now, so their time averages change. New statistics and new drop reasons add results, but they do not change the behavior.
