LinuxTcp has the receive buffer of Linux, and the window scale covers auto-tuning

LinuxTcp now has a receive buffer of 131072 bytes (receiveBufferSize), the tcp_rmem[1] that Linux gives each socket, so its window auto-tuning and Dynamic Right-Sizing act with its defaults. Before, LinuxTcp set no receive buffer, and its offered window stayed at advertisedWindow. Tcp keeps no receive buffer apart from the window.

With windowScalingFactor = -1, windowAutoTuning and a receive buffer that SO_RCVBUF does not lock, the window scale now also represents receiveBufferMax, as Linux tcp_select_initial_window() represents tcp_rmem[2]: with the default of 32 MiB, the shift is 10. Before, the shift represented only the window at the time of the SYN: a buffer of 131072 bytes got a shift of 1, and the offered window could not grow above 131070 bytes, although the buffer grew. A buffer that SO_RCVBUF locks keeps the shift of its own window. Simulations with LinuxTcp, and simulations that turn windowAutoTuning on with a receive buffer, change.
