The setup of a TCP connection lasts as long as its SYN retransmissions, and the stop of a node closes its connections

An active open of Tcp no longer fails after 75 s, and a passive open no longer leaves SYN_RCVD after 75 s. The SYN and SYN-ACK retransmissions alone limit the setup (the synRetries parameter, or TcpSocket::setSynRetries() for one connection), as in Linux. With the defaults of Tcp, 12 retransmissions take about 24 minutes; RFC 9293 section 3.8.3 asks for at least 3 minutes (MUST-23). LinuxTcp keeps the 11 s of Linux. When the retransmissions run out, an active open tells the application TCP_I_TIMED_OUT, as connect() of Linux fails with ETIMEDOUT, and a passive open returns to LISTEN, or closes if it was forked. Before, the connection aborted without an indication to the application, and a listener that does not fork closed. The timer that waits for the SEND of a deferred Fast Open SYN is now TcpConnection::deferredSynTimer, with the constant TCP_TIMEOUT_DEFERRED_SYN.

When a node stops, Tcp now sends a FIN on each connection that its application left open. Its stage of the stop ends when each connection has its data and its FIN acknowledged, or at stopOperationTimeout (2s by default). Before, Tcp deleted all connections at once, without a segment, so the peer kept a half-open connection. During this wait, Tcp ignores the commands of the applications and sends them nothing, because they are down already. stopOperationExtraTime now follows the wait in full, and a negative value no longer stops the run. Set stopOperationTimeout = 0s to delete the connections at once, as before. A crash does not change.

Simulations in which a SYN or a SYN-ACK gets no answer for more than 75 s, and simulations that stop nodes with open TCP connections, change. See the migration guide.
