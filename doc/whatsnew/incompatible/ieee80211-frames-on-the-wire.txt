IEEE 802.11 frames on the wire follow the standard

The IEEE 802.11 serializers now write and read the frames in the byte order and the layout of the standard. The QoS Control field uses the standard bit positions. Sequence Control and the numeric fields of management frames are little-endian. The Timestamp of Beacons and Probe Responses is the TSF in microseconds; before, it was the raw simulation time. The Association ID has its marker bits on the wire, and the MIB allocates the AIDs. ADDBA and DELBA use the standard parameter set layout. The A-MPDU delimiter keeps all 12 bits of the length and has a real CRC, and a delimiter with errors is marked incorrect. A-MSDU construction fills the transmitter address and the outer BSSID. The Block Ack Request is 20 bytes; before, it was 38 bytes. The BA Control field is little-endian, so a Compressed Block Ack is no longer read as Basic.

The PHY layer writes the legacy PHY headers (FHSS, DSSS and IR) and the SIGNAL field of the layered OFDM model on the wire. OFDM radios generate and check the SIGNAL parity and refuse undefined RATE codes, also in noncompliant operation. HR/DSSS and ERP transmissions carry their own PHY protocol identities.

Management frames keep more of their content. The model has the eosp bit of the data header, the Capability Information, the Listen Interval, all subfields of HT Capabilities and HT Operation, and the unmodeled information elements without a change. The deserializer creates the requested type of management body, checks the SSID and Supported Rates elements, and keeps the Current AP address of a Reassociation Request. Each data subtype deserializes, an action frame of an unmodeled category is kept as Ieee80211ActionFrameOther, and the body of a protected frame is not dissected. The legacy operational rate set is encoded apart from the HT and VHT rates.

AP beacon intervals are rounded down to whole TUs of 1024 us, for the schedule and for the advertisement: 100ms becomes 99.328ms. A configured interval must be from 1 to 65535 TUs. Beacon and Probe Response frames encode the DSSS Current Channel for 2.4 GHz, and their channelNumber field is a standard channel number, with -1 for an absent element; the radio channel parameters and the scan results stay internal indices. The mesh reason code for no forwarding information is 62, not 60. Non-QoS Data no longer shares the duplicate cache of QoS TID 0. The layered error models no longer give the tags of the transmitted packet to the received packet.

These corrections change packet bytes, some frame lengths and the timing, so the packet data fingerprints (~tND) and some trajectories of IEEE 802.11 simulations change. A model that makes its own frames should follow the migration guide.

The corrections of the management, data, Block Ack action and aggregation frames, of the PHY headers and of the error models were contributed by Miguel González-López.
