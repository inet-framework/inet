Mobile IPv6 signaling goes to the next hop, not to the whole link

Mobile Internet Protocol version 6 (Mobile IPv6, MIPv6) sent the Binding
Update (BU), the Care-of Test Init (CoTI), the Binding Acknowledgement (BA)
and the Binding Refresh Request on a fixed output interface without naming a
next hop, so the Ipv6 module addressed each of them to the link-layer
broadcast address. On 802.11 the access point relayed every such frame back
into its cell, and the relayed frame was sent without an acknowledgement.
Mipv6 now names the next hop, and the frame goes to that neighbour's
link-layer address when the Neighbour Cache holds it.

In MIPv6 simulations, the access point no longer relays the signaling into
the cell, and each signaling frame the access point forwards to a station
gets an 802.11 acknowledgement, so their trajectories and fingerprints may
change.
