The header and the data of a reception are decided separately

A flat receiver with an error model no longer decides the whole reception with one random draw and the SNIR of the whole reception. The decision of the header and the decision of the data each use the SNIR of their own time: a part fails if its SNIR is not above the SNIR threshold of the receiver, or else if one random draw against its error rate fails. The preamble needs only the SNIR threshold, the data needs a successful header, and the whole reception is successful if the data is. An interferer that starts during the data thus no longer changes the decision of the header. At a constant SNIR, the probability of a successful reception does not change. With the "mean" SNIR modes, interference over a part of the data now fails more frames, because the SNIR of the preamble and the header no longer raises the mean SNIR of the data. But a reception draws up to two random numbers instead of one, so the results of the wireless simulations with reception errors change. ApskReceiver, Ieee802154NarrowbandReceiver, and Ieee80211Receiver name the bits of their parts; another receiver that derives from FlatReceiverBase and has an error model must override computePartRange(). FlatReceiverBase no longer overrides computeIsReceptionSuccessful().
