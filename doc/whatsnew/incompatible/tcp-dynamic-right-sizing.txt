Dynamic Right-Sizing of the TCP receive buffer, as Linux

With windowAutoTuning and a receive buffer (receiveBufferSize), a TCP receiver now grows its buffer in a long transfer as Linux does (Dynamic Right-Sizing). At each read of the application after one receive RTT, the bytes read in that RTT become the receive space when they are more, and the buffer grows to twice the space, more during slow start, up to the new parameter receiveBufferMax (32 MiB, as tcp_rmem[2] of Linux). In the autoRead mode, each delivery to the application is a read. The new parameter receiveBufferLowRtt (1 ms, as tcp_rcvbuf_low_rtt) selects a smaller growth for small RTTs. The receiver estimates the receive RTT as Linux, with and without timestamps, and the status of a connection reports it with the receive space (rcvRtt, rcvSpace of TcpStatusInfo). Simulations that turn windowAutoTuning on with a receive buffer change, and LinuxTcp has a receive buffer by default (entry 53).
